# r1m3 — Deep Room (p1) vs The Apothecary (p2)

## Result

**Loss.** Final score **0 – 2**.

Deep Room lost all six. The Apothecary finished with Jynx and Raticate standing; its
Zapdos fainted to Muk's poison on the same turn as my last Pokémon, one residual tick
too late to matter.

Kills traded: I took **four of their six** (Arbok, Golbat, Gengar, Zapdos). They took
all six of mine (Rhydon, Hypno, Snorlax, Muk, Machamp, Slowbro). It went 39 turns of a
60-turn cap.

## The game plan, and what contact with the enemy did to it

The plan was the archetype's plan: put Trick Room up, spend those five turns moving
first with Pokémon that hit far above their Speed, and accept moving dead last in
between. Three rooms went up (turns 1, 6, 16) and all three worked exactly as designed:

- **Turn 0** was the best call I made. Jynx with a Focus Sash on a status team is a
  Lovely Kiss lead, and Trick Room's -7 priority means Slowbro would have been asleep
  before the room ever landed. I led the switch into Hypno instead — Insomnia — and
  their opening move failed outright.
- Room one bought Arbok (a clean 167-damage Earthquake) and chipped Jynx out of its sash.
- Room two bought Zapdos down 210 HP with No Guard Stone Edges that could not miss.
- Room three bought Golbat and set up Gengar.

Where it broke was not the room. It was **Toxic**. Zapdos poisoned every single
attacker I committed: Rhydon (t2), Machamp (t8), Snorlax (t26), Hypno (t30). My
Pokémon are enormous and slow, which normally means they absorb hits — but badly
poisoned, "absorbs hits" turns into "has three turns to live," and an escalating
toxic counter cannot be healed off a team with no cleric. By turn 34 my board looked
like five Pokémon and was really **two Pokémon and three zombies**: Snorlax at 59 with
a 66-point tick pending, Machamp at 31 with a 61-point tick pending. Both were
mathematically dead the moment they touched the field — each was worth exactly one
action, ever. That is the real damage a status team does to a bulky-offense team, and
I underrated it for the first fifteen turns.

The Apothecary also piloted its own theme well. Golbat's Roost stripping its Flying
type to blunt my Stone Edge, and its Air Slash flinch chain, cost me three turns in a
row around t13–15. That flinch lock is precisely what the room exists to break — a
flincher that moves second cannot flinch — and I was right to re-set the room over it.

## The turn that decided the match

**Turn 34.** Trick Room's last turn, Zapdos on 73 HP at -2 Defence from three Crunches,
and Snorlax — already dead to its own poison at the end of that turn — brought in over
Hypno's body as a free replacement to swing first. A Choice Band Body Slam at -2 Def
was roughly 188 into 73. It was a guaranteed kill on the Pokémon that had poisoned four
of my six.

They read it and **sacrificed Golbat**, throwing a 24-HP body in front of the shot.
Body Slam killed Golbat instead, Snorlax died to its toxin, and Zapdos walked away
alive to Roost back to full and Toxic-stall me for another five turns. I spent a
one-shot sniper and hit the wrong target. That single sacrifice is the match: had that
Body Slam landed on Zapdos, I was 4-3 up with their remainder being a 2-HP Jynx and a
self-burning Raticate that Slowbro hard-walls.

Honourable mention to the last turn: Discharge rolled **exactly 140** into Slowbro's
**exactly 140** HP. Zapdos died to Muk's poison in the same end-of-turn phase. A single
point of either HP or damage roll and the game continues 1-2 with both their survivors
in single-digit and double-digit HP.

## One thing I would do differently

**I never used Thunder Wave. Not once, in 39 turns, with 20 PP of it sitting on Hypno.**

I talked myself out of it early with a correct but far too narrow piece of reasoning:
paralysis halves Speed, and under Trick Room a slower foe moves *earlier*, so
paralysing things is actively counterproductive inside the room. That is true. But I
only held the room for **15 of 39 turns**. I spent roughly 24 turns *outside* it,
moving dead last into a team built around Jynx, Golbat, Gengar and Raticate — four
frail, fast Pokémon whose entire threat was acting before I did. Paralysis is the one
tool in my kit that patches the archetype's structural weakness in exactly the turns
the room cannot cover, and it comes with a 25% chance to blank a turn outright, which
is what actually beats a Roost staller.

The correct line was to spread paralysis on their fast frail attackers during the
off-room turns and save the room purely for the burst windows — the two effects cover
opposite halves of the game clock instead of competing. Second, smaller fix: I should
have kept Choice Band Snorlax — the only Pokémon I owned that out-damaged Roost — off
the field entirely until Zapdos was dead, rather than letting it eat a Toxic on turn 26
and become a one-action zombie.

## Postscript

The creed held up. The room did its job every time it was up: it beat a sleep lead, it
beat a flinch lock, it beat a Focus Sash, and it turned a 0-Speed-IV Brave Snorlax into
the fastest thing on the field three separate times. What beat Deep Room was not being
outsped. It was being outlasted — poisoned faster than a team with no healing can
recover, by an opponent who correctly identified that against Trick Room you do not
need to win the five turns, you only need to survive them.
