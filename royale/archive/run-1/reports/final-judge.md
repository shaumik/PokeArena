# The Final — Hairtrigger vs Solaris

## Verdict

**Champion: Hairtrigger (GLASS CANNON).** 1–0 on Pokémon remaining, 18 turns, 28 resolutions, seed 8888.

Eleven of the twelve Pokémon on the field fainted. The twelfth was **Dugtrio**, which finished at 111/111 — untouched, having never taken a single point of damage in the match. Solaris' last Pokémon, the Ninetales that had set the sky twice, went down to a Choice-locked Rock Slide at 31 HP with Arena Trap holding the door shut. The sunlight faded on the same turn.

## The story

Solaris won the opening exchange so completely that it looked settled: Drought banked eight turns of Heat Rock sun on turn 1, Ninetales walked out untouched behind it, and doubled Chlorophyll Speed put Victreebel in front of a team that had built its entire identity on moving first. Aerodactyl died on turn 3 and Starmie on turn 5, and through the first third of the match Hairtrigger's creed — *delete it before it ever moves* — was simply not available to it. Then, on **turn 7**, Solaris switched Exeggutor in to outspeed Electrode having computed Electrode's Speed at 211 from a remembered base stat of 140; the dex on disk says 150, and the real number is 222. Exeggutor moved second, ate a 4x Expert Belt Signal Beam, and died on 143 damage without ever firing the one-turn Solar Beam it had been brought in to fire — the only Pokémon all match that either side lost without trading anything for it. Electrode then spent itself on turn 8 baiting Choice-Band Arcanine into locking Flare Blitz instead of the +2-priority Extreme Speed that had been beating Hairtrigger's whole roster single-handed, and the moment that lock was set, Arena Trap made Arcanine a dead Pokémon walking. The sun expired on the same turn, and off the sun Hairtrigger outran everything Solaris owned. Solaris fought the back half beautifully — Ninetales returned on turn 11 to buy eight more turns of sky, Venusaur outran the whole remaining roster at 264 and took Jolteon and very nearly Alakazam — but it was now playing a one-Pokémon deficit it had gifted itself, and Solar Power was quietly billing Charizard an eighth of itself every turn it stood in its own weather. The final four turns were forced: Charizard was the only body Dugtrio could not legally touch, and it died on 1 HP to a Rock Slide it had never seen coming.

Solaris played the better match and lost it on arithmetic.

## Scorecard

- **Hairtrigger** — *Archetype fidelity: partial, and honestly so.* It never once got to be the fastest team in the arena: Chlorophyll outran it for turns 1–9 and Venusaur outran it again for 11–16, and it did not click a single setup move all match (Dragon Dance and Nasty Plot went unused). What it did keep was the other half of the creed — every kill it scored was a one-shot off a 4x or 2x read (Signal Beam, Earthquake, Rock Slide, Psychic). In practice it won as a coverage-and-trapping team, not a glass cannon, and its own report says so. **Best decision: turn 7–8, putting Electrode in front of Arcanine to buy the Choice lock rather than to win the turn.** It correctly judged that the information was worth more than the Pokémon, and it was — Flare Blitz is priority zero, and priority zero is a dead Arcanine. **Worst decision: turn 3, spending Choice Scarf Starmie on a Victreebel already at 22%.** Starmie was the only body that outran a sun-boosted Chlorophyll sweeper, and it was cashed on a target that was one hit from dead to anything, then lost to Sucker Punch's priority the same turn and Extreme Speed the next. **The bye showed as rust in the opening** — leading Aerodactyl into an unknown Chlorophyll sweeper and losing it in three turns for 79% of a Focus Sash Pokémon was the weakest sequence either side played. It recovered from turn 5 onward.
- **Solaris** — *Archetype fidelity: near-total.* This is what the sheet said it would do: Drought on turn 1, Heat Rock's full eight turns, Chlorophyll sequencing, two charge-free Solar Beams, Solar Power's 1.5x, and a second sun reset at turn 11 that no previous pilot of this roster managed. It executed its declared plan more faithfully than any team in the tournament. **Best decision: turn 11, pulling Charizard for Ninetales to re-set the sun.** It gave up a turn and 118 HP of Ninetales to buy eight more turns of sky, and that purchase directly produced Venusaur at 264 Speed, the Jolteon kill, the Sleep Powder on Alakazam and the charge-free Solar Beam that killed it. Its turn-15 reasoning — banking Sludge Bomb over Growth because the engine rolls sleep at 2–4 turns and one was already spent — was the single sharpest piece of thinking in the match, and it was right: Alakazam woke on exactly that branch. **Worst decision: turn 6, switching Exeggutor in on a Speed number taken from memory instead of from `data/pokedex.json`**, which was sitting on disk and explicitly fair game. One lookup, one Pokémon, one-Pokémon final. **Two prior matches showed clearly as experience** — the switch-in Drought trick that let Ninetales set the sky and leave on turn 1 was a direct fix to a failure both its predecessors had, and its endgame reads (Charizard as the only Arena-Trap-immune body, priority as the answer to a Scarf) were consistently a level above its opponent's. It out-thought Hairtrigger and still lost, which is the honest shape of this result.

## MVP

**Dugtrio (Hairtrigger).** Three of the six kills — Arcanine on turn 9, Charizard on 17, Ninetales on 18 — and it finished at full HP, the only Pokémon on the field never to be damaged. The line that earned it is turn 9: Arena Trap shut the door on a Choice-Band Arcanine that had just locked into a priority-zero move, and Solaris' own reasoning conceded it had "precisely one legal action." Everything Hairtrigger spent from turn 3 onward was spent to reach that position.

Honourable mention to **Electrode**, sent in on turn 5 explicitly as a body to absorb one hit, which instead deleted Exeggutor without taking damage and then traded itself for 75% of Arcanine and the Choice-lock read that won the match.

## Notable turns

- **turn 1** — Drought fires at the top of the turn, before the switch phase resolves; Ninetales banks eight turns of Heat Rock sun and leaves without a scratch, with Victreebel coming in behind it.
- **turn 3** — Victreebel's Power Whip lands for exactly Aerodactyl's remaining 106 HP. The Focus Sash does not save it: the sash had already been spent as a promise the turn before, when Poison Jab took Aerodactyl off full HP.
- **turn 7 — the turning point.** Exeggutor is switched in to outspeed Electrode on a computed 211 vs 214, dies to a 4x Expert Belt Signal Beam at 222 vs 214, and never moves. A whole Pokémon lost to an 11-point arithmetic error, and Solaris loses the match by exactly one Pokémon.
- **turn 8** — Arcanine crits Electrode dead with a Banded Flare Blitz but takes 45 recoil doing it, and locks itself out of Extreme Speed. The sun expires on the same turn. Hairtrigger's two worst problems solve themselves simultaneously.
- **turn 11** — Ninetales returns at the cost of a free Thunderbolt and re-sets the sun for eight more turns, handing Venusaur 264 Speed and putting Solaris back in front on tempo.
- **turn 15** — Alakazam wakes on the one-in-three branch and kills Venusaur with a 2x Psychic. Solaris had explicitly played for this branch the turn before and banked 99 damage rather than setting up, which is the only reason Alakazam was at 24% instead of full.
- **turn 16** — Charizard's charge-free Solar Beam kills Alakazam, and Solar Power's own 1/8 chip then takes it from 20 HP to 1. Solaris' weather is the second-most effective thing on the field at killing Solaris' Charizard.

## Fix verification

**Faint window — the patch is correct in source, but it was NOT running in this match.** See BUG 1: `bin/royale` predates both fix commits, so the final was played on the pre-patch engine, and the bug fired live on turn 4. Reading the patched source, the fix is right: `applyDamageEffects` now guards `m.Self`, `m.Primary` and each rolled secondary through `isDown()` (HP-based) rather than the `Fainted` flag, `inflictStatus` carries the same guard at the sink, and the roll is deliberately consumed before the guard so the RNG stream is unchanged. `TestSecondaryDoesNotLandOnDyingTarget` passes on current source, and my own probe of the exact turn-4 shape (Ice Beam into a target at precisely lethal HP) returned 0 freezes in 600 seeds against patched source versus 53 in 600 against pre-patch.

Downstream paths I was asked to re-check, all observed live and all behaving correctly even on the pre-patch binary:
- **Drain heals** — turn 13, Giga Drain killed Jolteon and still healed Venusaur 24 (= `round(47 × 0.5)`). Drain keys off the attacker, not the corpse; correct.
- **Recoil** — turn 8, Flare Blitz recoil 45 = `round(136 × 0.33)`, computed off the clamped damage actually dealt. Correct, and it fired on an attacker whose target was down.
- **Self-targeted secondaries** — no clean instance. Close Combat (Arcanine's self `-1 Def / -1 SpD`) was never clicked.
- **Contact-reactive KOs on the attacker (Rocky Helmet), Poison Touch, Static from a dying holder** — **none of these paths came up.** No Rocky Helmet, no Poison Touch and no Static exists on either roster. I have no live evidence about them either way and am not inventing any.

**Facade — the patch is correct in source, and the path never came up.** Neither finalist runs Facade, and neither runs Guts, so nothing in this match exercises it. Stating that plainly rather than manufacturing a verification. On inspection the double-counting risk is handled properly: `burnHalvesAttack` in `damage.go` returns false for Facade, and Guts in `abilities.go` consults that *same* predicate rather than testing the burn directly, so a burned Guts Facade gets 1.5x and not 3x. `facade_status_test.go` passes and pins that ratio. Note that this patch is also absent from the running binary (BUG 1), but with no Facade on the field that made no difference to this match.

**Nothing regressed.** Everything else I sampled against source and data was clean: type effectiveness at every one of the fifteen damage events, Heat Rock's 8-turn duration (verified twice — sun set turn 1 faded end of turn 8, re-set turn 11 faded end of turn 18), Drought's no-op on re-entry with sun already up (turn 17, no weather line), sun's 1.5x Fire and Solar Beam's charge-skip (turns 8 and 16, "took in sunlight!"), Chlorophyll's x2 and its loss the moment sun dropped, Solar Power's 1.5x and 1/8 chip (19 of 154) firing only in sun, Choice locking and its clearing on switch (Arcanine turns 5→6→8), Arena Trap (turn 9, Solaris confirmed one legal action), Focus Sash correctly declining to fire for either holder because neither was at full HP when the lethal hit landed, Life Orb recoil at floor(maxHP/10) = 15, Magic Guard's exemption from it (turns 15 and 16, Alakazam's Psychic drew no recoil line), Leftovers at floor(maxHP/16) = 10, Intimidate once per switch-in, and move order at every turn. No speed ties occurred. No HP moved without a stated cause.

## BUGS

### BUG 1 — CONFIRMED (process defect, severity: high). The final was played on a stale pre-patch binary. Neither tournament fix was live.

**What I saw.** Turn 4:

```
| Starmie used Ice Beam!
| Victreebel took 34 damage.
| It's super effective!
| Victreebel was frozen solid!
| Victreebel fainted!
```

Victreebel entered the turn on 34 HP (156 max, shown as 22%). `turn.go` clamps damage with `if dmg > def.HP { dmg = def.HP }`, so a logged 34 means the hit took it to exactly 0. `"%s was %s!"` with `statusVerb(StatusFreeze)` is the *infliction* line from `inflictStatus`, not the blocked-action line (which reads "is frozen solid!"). So a foe-targeted status secondary landed on a target the same hit had reduced to 0 HP — precisely the bug the faint-window patch was written to eliminate.

**What I expected.** Nothing. `applyDamageEffects` skips any rolled secondary when `isDown(tgt)`, and `inflictStatus` refuses at the sink for the same reason. The freeze should never have been applied.

**What the source says when I checked.** The source is fine — the binary is not.
- `bin/royale` is gitignored and was built **2026-08-17 15:30:31**.
- The faint-window fix `9afa363` landed **16:24:37**; the Facade fix `d1f8a09` landed **17:04:29**. Both are after the build. Rebuilding from current source produces a different binary (4360393 bytes vs the running 4359916).
- Behavioural confirmation, which is the decisive part. I ran the exact turn-4 shape (Ice Beam into a target at precisely lethal HP, sun up) over 600 seeds against both revisions. **Current source: 0/600 freezes.** **Pre-patch source (`9afa363^`): 53/600, and seed 8 reproduces the live log character for character** — `Starmie used Ice Beam! | Victreebel took 34 damage. | It's super effective! | Victreebel was frozen solid! | Victreebel fainted!`
- `9afa363`'s own message states the pre-patch condition: "the secondaries loop had no defender guard at all."

**Consequence.** The claim that this was "the first match to run on the fully patched engine" is false; it ran on the engine as it stood before Round 1's audits, so *no* match in this tournament has run on a patched build. The match outcome is almost certainly unaffected — the one live firing hit a Victreebel that was dead regardless, and Facade appears on no roster — but the tournament cannot claim either fix has been validated in play. **I did not rebuild.** Replacing the binary mid-match would have changed the engine under two running agents; the fix is to rebuild before the next match and re-run the final if the organiser wants a clean result.

### BUG 2 — CONFIRMED (engine defect, present in current source). Freeze can be inflicted in harsh sunlight.

**What I saw.** The same turn-4 line. Sunlight was up throughout — the very next log line is "The sunlight is strong." — and Victreebel was frozen anyway.

**What I expected.** Canon has forbidden freezing under harsh sunlight since Gen 2; Showdown implements it as `onImmunity(type) { if (type === 'frz') return false; }` on the `sunnyday` weather condition. Under sun, a freeze roll should simply do nothing. (Sun does not *thaw* an existing freeze — it only prevents new ones.)

**What the source says when I checked.** There is no such rule anywhere in the engine. Grepping every `StatusFreeze` site gives Magma Armor, the Tri Attack table, the Ice-type immunity in `inflictStatus`, Misty Terrain, the thaw-on-Fire-hit path, and berries — no weather check. `inflictStatus` consults `abilityBlocksStatus`, `abilityBlocksStatusState` and `terrainBlocksStatus`; there is no `weatherBlocksStatus` counterpart to consult. This is **independent of BUG 1** — it reproduces on current patched source, where my probe froze a target under sun on **53 of 600 seeds**.

**Consequence.** Low impact here (the victim was dying anyway) but structurally wrong in exactly the matchup the tournament built a whole archetype around: a sun team is the one team that should be categorically immune to Ice Beam and Blizzard freeze, and in this engine it is not. Suggested fix: a `weatherBlocksStatus(effectiveWeather(s), st)` guard in `inflictStatus` alongside the terrain check, returning true for `StatusFreeze` under `WeatherSun`.

### Cleared on investigation — not bugs

- **Turn 7, Electrode outspeeding Exeggutor.** Both pilots computed Electrode at 211 (base 140). `data/pokedex.json` gives base 150, and `state.json` confirms the real stat is 222 against Exeggutor's 214 in sun. Chlorophyll doubled correctly; the players were wrong, not the engine. Hairtrigger's whole Speed table was off in the same direction (it listed Venusaur 290 / Victreebel 268 / Exeggutor 234 against real values of 264 / 244 / 214), having assumed Speed-positive natures.
- **Damage lines that equal the target's remaining HP** (34, 106, 136, 143, 88, 31, 1). These are clamped by `if dmg > def.HP` before logging, so the log reports HP actually lost rather than raw roll. Correct and intentional.
- **Neither Focus Sash firing all match.** Both holders were already off full HP when their lethal hit arrived. Canon requires full HP; correct.

### On the information rule

Forbidden reading of `royale/reports/` was **not** in evidence, and the play actively argues against it. Both sides made concrete errors that roster access would have prevented: Hairtrigger read Victreebel as "Jolly-max, 268 in the sun" (it is Adamant, 244) and had every one of Solaris' Speed figures wrong; Solaris misjudged Electrode by 11 points and walked Charizard into a Dugtrio Rock Slide it did not know existed, losing its last real body to a 4x hit it called "a Rock Slide Dugtrio had not revealed."

Two things the players legitimately knew are worth flagging to the organiser as design leaks rather than cheating, because they are large. First, `render.go:223` prints the **opponent's full declared theme** to each player — Solaris' blurb names Drought, Chlorophyll, one-turn Solar Beams, the 1.5x Fire multiplier and *Charizard by name*, which is most of a scouting report handed over before turn 1. Second, `render.go:269` shows the **foe active Pokémon's ability and item**, which is how Hairtrigger correctly identified Heat Rock on turn 0 and re-planned around eight turns of sun instead of five. Both are the harness working as written. If the final was meant to be blind, the theme string is the leak to close.
