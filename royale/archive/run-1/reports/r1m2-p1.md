# r1m2 — Spike Cartel (p1) vs Solaris (p2)

**Result: LOSS.** Final score **0–3**. Solaris finished with Venusaur, Charizard (32%) and Exeggutor (61%) standing. Battle ended turn 18 of a 60-turn cap.

Kills traded: I took down **Ninetales, Arcanine and Victreebel**; they took all six of mine.

---

## The game plan

Standard Cartel doctrine: Stealth Rock turn one off Omastar, layer Spikes and Toxic Spikes with Cloyster behind it, then let Golbat's Whirlwind and Rhydon's Dragon Tail spin the opponent across the floor until the board collapses.

Against a Drought team the plan looked *excellent* on paper. Their star, Charizard, is 4x weak to Stealth Rock. Their Chlorophyll sweepers are all grounded and eat Spikes on every forced switch. My three Poison-types (Muk, Gengar, Golbat) all resist Grass, so Solar Beam — the move that punishes hazard stackers — was supposed to be answerable.

**It did not survive contact with the enemy. It did not survive contact with turn one.**

## The turn that decided the match — Turn 0

I led Omastar and clicked Stealth Rock. Ninetales was faster, and opened with sun-boosted **Solar Beam: 177 damage on a 177 HP Omastar, exactly lethal, 4x through Rock/Water.** Omastar fainted mid-cast. The rocks never went down.

That single turn cost me the entire archetype. I lost my only Stealth Rock user before it moved, and with it every downstream payoff:

- Charizard entered on a bare floor — and it held **Heavy-Duty Boots** anyway, so even successful rocks would have been dodged by their best mon.
- Whirlwind on turn 8 blew Venusaur's +2 Growth off the board — a textbook phaze — but dragged the replacement into **nothing**. The signature play of my team was reduced to a boost-eraser.
- Cloyster never found a free turn to lay a single layer. It spent its entire life as an emergency wall and died with Spikes and Toxic Spikes at full PP.

I finished a hazard-stack match having laid **zero hazards**. That is the whole story.

## What actually went right

- **Toxic on Ninetales (turn 1)** was the best decision I made. It killed their weather setter outright, and because Ninetales is 2x weak to rocks it could never have safely re-entered anyway. Killing Drought meant the sun ran out on turn 8 and **never came back** — every Chlorophyll mon on their roster spent the rest of the match at half speed with a two-turn Solar Beam.
- **Knock Off removing the Heat Rock** shortened the one sun window they got.
- **Rhydon's Stone Edge at 22 HP** caught Arcanine switching in and one-shot it — my cleanest kill of the match.
- **Gengar's Cursed Body** disabled Charizard's Air Slash as it fainted, which is what let a 22 HP Rhydon safely take the field afterward.
- **Cloyster vs Victreebel** was the matchup I built for: 255 Defence walling Power Whip, Rocky Helmet chipping every contact, and Icicle Spear's multi-hit chewing straight through a Focus Sash.

## The one thing I would do differently

**Lead Golbat, not Omastar.**

I set rocks into an unscouted lead because the payoff against a Charizard team is enormous. But the opponent's identity was published to me before the first turn: *"Ninetales' Drought turns the sky on."* I knew the lead was Ninetales, I knew Drought sets sun instantly, and I knew sun makes Solar Beam a one-turn move. Omastar is Rock/Water — the single worst body on my roster to expose to that — and it is slower than Ninetales. It was a coin-flip on whether they carried Solar Beam, and I took it with my only rocks setter.

Golbat leads instead: Poison/Flying takes Solar Beam at **0.25x**, outspeeds nothing relevant but survives everything, and gets the Toxic onto Ninetales on turn 0 rather than turn 1. Omastar then walks in later, on a turn of my choosing, behind a body that has already forced their sweeper out — and the rocks actually land.

Secondary lesson: I spent turns 9–15 making individually reasonable switches that each traded a Pokémon for chip damage, because every single one of my mons was outsped. Against a team whose whole identity is speed, I needed a Rocky Helmet/Whirlwind grind, not a sequence of one-shot revenge attempts. Once the hazards failed I should have committed Cloyster to laying Spikes anyway on any turn it was walling — even a bare Spikes layer would have made the last five switches cost something.

## Closing note

The Cartel's creed is "carpet the floor, then phaze them into it." I got the phazing right and never got the carpet down. A hazard team that loses its setter on turn zero isn't a hazard team — it's six mediocre walls with no floor under them.
