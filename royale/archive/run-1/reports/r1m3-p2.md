# r1m3 — The Apothecary (p2) vs Deep Room (p1)

## Result

**WIN — The Apothecary.** Opponent wiped 6-0 on turn 39 of 60.

Final score: **2 Pokémon remaining to 0.**

- **Standing:** Jynx (1/141), Raticate (115/131, burned)
- **Fallen:** Arbok, Gengar, Golbat, Zapdos
- **Deep Room:** Rhydon, Hypno, Machamp, Muk, Snorlax, Slowbro — all fainted

Of their six, **four died with my status still in them**: Rhydon (toxic), Machamp
(toxic), Snorlax (toxic), Muk (burn). A fifth, Hypno, died poisoned. Only Slowbro
died clean, and it died to a 2x Discharge from a bird that was already dead.

## Game plan, and whether it survived contact

The plan was the creed: put something on everything, then let the arithmetic do the
killing. Against a **Trick Room** team it needed one immediate amendment.

**Paralysis was cut from the playbook on turn 1.** Glare and Thunder Wave halve the
target's Speed — against a roster deliberately built with 0 Speed IVs and Speed-cutting
natures, that is *helping them*, because under Trick Room the slowest move first. The
only thing paralysis offered here was the 25% full-para roll, which is not worth a turn.
So Thunder Wave was never clicked once in 39 turns. **Toxic, Will-O-Wisp and Confuse Ray
did the entire job** — statuses whose value is independent of the turn order.

That amendment turned out to be the match. A Trick Room team gets five fast turns and
then moves dead last; it is built to win races. Poison does not race. It collects the
same rent inside the room and outside it, and it kept collecting while their Pokémon
hid on the bench — Machamp sat at 14% from turn 12 to turn 37 because the toxic I gave
it never washed off, and it died the moment they ran out of places to put it.

**Where the plan bent:**

- **Insomnia walled the scalpel.** Their Hypno was a dedicated sleep-blocker, and my
  Lovely Kiss failed on turn 1. Jynx's sleep never landed all match. The team had a
  second and third answer, so this cost tempo rather than the game.
- **Facade does not double off my own burn in this engine.** I ran the numbers back
  from real damage rolls on turn 19 — Guts *was* applying (Attack 199), but Facade was
  still firing at 70 base power, not 140. Raticate was roughly half as threatening as
  the archetype advertises. That discovery on turn 19 is the only reason I pulled it
  out of a Snorlax Body Slam instead of trading it away for 109 damage.
- **Hex found a better job than the one it was hired for.** Hex is supposed to double
  against my own status. It did that once, on a burned Muk. Its actual match-winning
  use was that **Ghost is 2x on their two Psychic pivots** — an unboosted Hex hit
  Slowbro for 141 on turn 24 and set up the exact kill 15 turns later.

## The turn that decided the match

**Turn 34 — sacrificing Golbat.**

Trick Room's last turn. Snorlax had switched, so its Choice Band lock had reset and it
could pick freely. Zapdos was at 79/197 with Defense at **-2** from repeated Crunches:
dead to either Band move. Raticate at 115 was dead to Body Slam. Everything I owned
lost that turn.

Golbat was at 24 HP and paralyzed — and paralysis had cut its Speed to 27, which is
*slower than their entire roster*. It could never move first again, which meant it
could never Roost again, which meant it had no future at all. So I fed it to the
Body Slam.

The bill came due on the same turn it died: Snorlax's toxic ticked for 59 and it
fainted in the same message. **One body traded, one body collected, and Zapdos and
Raticate both walked away intact** — 3-3 instead of 3-4 with my wall dead. Zapdos
then went on to personally kill Hypno, Muk, Machamp and Slowbro.

The pretty turn was the last one — a 3 HP Zapdos, poisoned and guaranteed to die at
end of turn, spending its final action on a 2x Discharge that hit Slowbro for exactly
140 into exactly 140 HP. But that shot only existed because of turn 34.

## One thing I would do differently

**Turn 25: I clicked Will-O-Wisp on Snorlax instead of Sludge Wave, and it cost me
Gengar.**

Gengar was at 33 HP, immune to the Body Slam that Snorlax had shown twice, and I
reasoned that burning their Choice Band breaker protected my whole roster. But
Will-O-Wisp is 85% and Sludge Wave is 100%. It missed, Snorlax revealed Crunch, and my
best attacker died having thrown exactly two Hexes.

The error was not the target — it was reaching for a coin-flip status when the patient
was *already* going to be handled and I had a guaranteed 91 damage available. Sludge
Wave twice kills that Snorlax. My whole archetype is built on effects that are certain
(Toxic's ticks, a burn's halving, Hex's doubling); I should not spend my one turn of
safety on the one bottle in the bag with a 15% chance of being empty. **Status first
is the creed, but not status at 85% when damage at 100% closes the same account.**

Zapdos ended up killing four Pokémon by itself and had to do it precisely because
Gengar wasn't there. It should not have had to.
