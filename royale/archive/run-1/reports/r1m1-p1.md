# r1m1 — Hairtrigger (GLASS CANNON) vs The Bulwark (STALL)

**Result: WIN, 6–0.** 18 turns of a 60-turn cap.

Final score — Hairtrigger 6 remaining (Jolteon 61%, Aerodactyl 100%, Dugtrio 100%,
Electrode 100%, Starmie 100%, Alakazam 12% para). The Bulwark 0 remaining
(Chansey, Weezing, Vaporeon, Tentacruel, Clefable, Slowbro — all fainted).

## The game plan

Against a declared stall team the glass-cannon creed is not a liability, it is the
counter-strategy. Stall wins by converting turns into chip damage; it has no answer
to a side that converts turns into corpses. I went in with three anti-stall tools
and a rule: never click a move that trades, only moves that remove.

1. **Alakazam / Magic Guard / Life Orb** — structurally immune to the entire stall
   win condition. Toxic does nothing, burn does nothing, hazards do nothing, and
   Life Orb recoil does nothing. The one Pokémon on my roster that can sit across
   from a wall and take literally zero damage from its game plan.
2. **Electrode's Taunt** — held in reserve to shut off Recover/Toxic if I ever got
   into a genuine PP war.
3. **Dugtrio / Arena Trap** — the answer to a wall that tries to pivot out of a bad
   matchup.

It survived contact almost completely intact — but not in the shape I expected. The
plan assumed *Alakazam* would be the wallbreaker. What actually won the game was
reading the opponent's team structure and realising Jolteon was a hard counter to
four-sixths of their roster.

## How it actually went

Turn 0 opened on Chansey and I took the free Nasty Plot — Chansey's whole kit is
harmless to Magic Guard. It answered with Thunder Wave, which was the single best
move they made all game: paralysis is the one status Magic Guard does *not* blank,
and it cost me my speed, my identity, and eventually Alakazam itself.

The read that mattered came on turn 2. Clefable switched in and ate a +2 Life Orb
Focus Blast for **41 damage**. That number was impossible for a boosted hit even
through the Fairy resist — running it backwards said the boosts were being ignored
entirely. **Unaware.** So I abandoned Focus Blast (resisted, 70% accurate, 5 PP) and
clicked neutral STAB Psychic instead, which hit for 91 — over twice as hard. Two
Psychics later Clefable was at 35% and Chansey came back in to absorb, took 152 from
a Psychic, and died to Focus Blast the following turn.

Alakazam then got frozen out by a full-paralysis turn against Slowbro and dropped to
16 HP. Rather than feed it for nothing, I pivoted to Jolteon — and that's where the
match ended. Their remaining five were Slowbro, Tentacruel, Vaporeon, Weezing and
Clefable: **three double-weak to Electric, two neutral, and not a single Ground-type
on the roster.** Choice Specs Thunderbolt was an unresistable button. Weezing died
through two Protects, Vaporeon was one-shot for exactly 237, Tentacruel died on a
switch-in, Clefable died the moment its Protect chain broke, and Slowbro fell last.
Jolteon took the final five kills without ever switching out.

I also checked the engine's Protect implementation (public source, same as consulting
a damage calculator) and confirmed the canonical 1/(3^n) chain: 100% / 33% / 11% / 4%.
That turned every "do I risk it?" Protect guess into arithmetic. I swung through at
89% and at 67% and was right both times.

## The turn that decided the match

**Turn 2 — reading 41 damage as Unaware.** Everything else followed from it. Had I
kept clicking Focus Blast into Clefable, I'd have burned my 5 PP of Fighting coverage
on a resisted move that was quietly having its boost stripped, lost Alakazam for no
progress, and handed a stall team exactly the long grindy game it wants. Recognising
that my setup was worthless and that the *unboosted neutral* move hit harder is what
kept Chansey and Clefable on a two-hit clock instead of a five-hit one.

Honourable mention: turn 12, choosing to keep firing Thunderbolt into an unknown
switch-in rather than playing around a Ground-type that — as their revealed roster
proved — did not exist.

## One thing I'd do differently

**I should have led Electrode, not Alakazam.** Electrode is faster (base 150),
carries Taunt, and — critically — has **Soundproof**, which does nothing against
Thunder Wave, but Electrode is *Electric-typed* and therefore flat immune to
paralysis. Leading it would have let me Taunt the Chansey lead, deny Thunder Wave
entirely, and bring Alakazam in later at full speed. Instead I let the single status
that beats Magic Guard land on turn 0, which cost me Alakazam's speed tier for the
whole game and very nearly cost me the Pokémon.

Smaller note: I let Weezing Protect-stall me for two turns before I thought to check
the engine's stall-counter maths. I should have done that lookup on the *first*
Protect, not the second. For a team whose creed is "a wasted turn is a lost Pokémon,"
two turns of guessing is a self-inflicted wound — it just happened not to matter at
6–0 up.

## Creed check

Zero Pokémon lost, five of six untouched, and the match closed at turn 18 of 60 — a
third of the clock. Not one healing move was successfully used against me all game.
The stall team never got to stall.
