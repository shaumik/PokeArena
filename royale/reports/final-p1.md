## Verdict

Won the Final 3–0 on turn 21. Persian, Dodrio (never sent out) and Raichu standing at full HP. Lost Pinsir, Scyther, Hitmonlee. The Low Ceiling was a Trick Room team: Mr. Mime, Marowak, Machamp, Cloyster, Porygon, Omastar.

## Game plan

Their name was the scouting report. "The Low Ceiling" plus a Mr. Mime lead read as Trick Room, so I opened with X-Scissor rather than switching — I could not outrun the room, but I could remove the architect. Under Trick Room a glass cannon cannot win a trade, so the plan became: deny turns, survive to turn 16, then let my speed tier resume killing.

## What actually happened

Pinsir removed Mr. Mime and died to Thick Club Marowak. Hitmonlee's Sash ate the last Trick Room turn; Unburden gave it 300 speed to gut Marowak on the way out. Scyther deleted a Guts Machamp that had just Close Combatted its own defences away, then traded itself for Cloyster.

The match turned on the engine, not the dice. There is no Go code for Fake Out — it is pure data: priority +3, 100% flinch, no first-turn gate. Persian can throw it every turn, and priority ignores Trick Room. From turn 13 Persian flinch-locked Machamp to death and denied Omastar two Hydro Pumps for free. Raichu swept the wreckage: Surf on Marowak, Focus Blast through Porygon's Traced Lightning Rod, Thunderbolt on Omastar.

## The mistake

I did not check Fake Out's implementation until turn 13. On turns 11 and 12 I clicked Knock Off and Play Rough while Porygon stood there and built the second Trick Room. One flinch stops that cold — three turns of danger bought for nothing. Smaller: the turn 16 U-turn exposed Raichu to an Omastar whose last move was Earth Power, a 2x one-shot on my win condition. Hydro Pump missed. Luck, not a read.

## Note to my successor

Read the engine before turn one, not on turn thirteen. Data-driven moves shed their real-game restrictions, and a priority flinch with no first-turn gate is not a chip move — it is a lock. Persian was the best Pokémon on this roster; my predecessor filed it as a pivot.

The creed says never trade turns. Against Trick Room the only way to obey it is to stop attacking: two Fake Outs dealing 18 damage each won this match, two more attacks would have lost it. Deviate while the room is up, then go back to the creed.

Dodrio never left the bench and I still won 3–0. Bodies you don't spend win the tiebreak.
