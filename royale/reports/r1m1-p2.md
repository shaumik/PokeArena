# r1m1 — The Bulwark (p2) vs Hairtrigger (p1)

## Result

**Loss. 0–6.** Hairtrigger finished with all six Pokémon alive; I lost Chansey, Clefable, Weezing, Tentacruel, Vaporeon and Slowbro. Battle ended turn 18 of a possible 60 — a stall team never got within forty turns of the cap it was built to reach.

Damage inflicted across the whole match: two Scalds and one Moonblast. Their Alakazam finished at 12% and their Jolteon at 60%. I did not remove a single body.

## Game plan, and what happened to it

The plan was the creed: land Toxic early, pivot to whichever wall answered the current attacker, heal, and let the turn cap do the killing. Against a glass cannon that should be a favourable matchup — frail attackers die to poison chip while my recovery outpaces their damage.

It survived contact for about four turns, then broke on two things I could not have pre-scouted and one I should have handled better.

1. **Alakazam had Magic Guard.** My entire primary win condition — Toxic — did literally nothing to their lead. I adapted correctly here: Thunder Wave instead of Toxic, then Clefable's Unaware to blank a Nasty Plot, then Seismic Toss as Magic-Guard-proof fixed damage. That part of the game I am happy with. Paralysis later bought me a free turn at exactly the right moment.
2. **Jolteon had Choice Specs and 162 special attack.** This was the match. I ran the numbers off the first hit: Specs Thunderbolt dealt 138–163 to a neutral target. My roster's special-defence spread meant **every single Pokémon I owned was a one-shot** — Slowbro 251–296 into 202 HP, Vaporeon 216–255 into 237, Clefable 81–96 into a 70-HP Clefable, Tentacruel 132–156 into 94. Only a *full-health* Weezing (140–165 into 172) and a *full-health* Clefable (96 into 202, out-healed by a 101 Soft-Boiled) survived it, and by the time Jolteon arrived neither was at full health.
3. **Nothing I had outsped it.** My fastest was Tentacruel at 120 against Jolteon's ~200. Every wall I sent in died *before it was allowed to act*. From turn 12 onward I could not deal damage at all — not because I picked wrong moves, but because no move of mine ever resolved. That is the specific nightmare for a stall team: not being out-damaged, but being denied the turn.

Once I understood that, I played the only currency left — turns — correctly: I chained Protects, and I deliberately switched to reset the Protect counter (it resets on switch-out, restoring the 100% first-use rate) to squeeze out extra guaranteed refusals. I also triaged on the right axis, spending Tentacruel first because it was the only survivor with no recovery move and therefore could never anchor a stall. None of it mattered, but none of it was wrong.

## The turn that decided the match

**Turn 4 — Chansey used Seismic Toss instead of Soft-Boiled.**

Alakazam was at 55% with roughly 72 HP. Seismic Toss deals a flat 100. I saw a guaranteed kill and took it. Chansey was at 227/357 and I had a 178-HP heal available; I judged +1 Focus Blast at around 199 and decided Chansey lived through it. Focus Blast rolled 227 — exactly lethal — and Chansey died with the KO still queued behind it.

That is the hinge. Chansey was my special sponge with 357 HP, the one Pokémon whose bulk could have absorbed Specs Thunderbolts (Thunderbolt into Chansey's 126 SpD and 357 HP is a comfortable three-hit, and Soft-Boiled restores 178 a turn). With Chansey alive, Jolteon does not sweep — it gets walled and eventually Toxic'd or Seismic Tossed to death. I traded my Jolteon answer for an Alakazam I had already neutralised with paralysis, before I even knew Jolteon existed.

I broke my own creed on that turn. "Never trade, always heal." I traded.

## One thing I would do differently

Heal on turn 4. More generally: **as the stall team, when a heal and a kill are both available and the kill is not lethal-this-turn-guaranteed-from-full, take the heal.** My archetype does not need to win the current exchange; it wins by still being there in twenty turns. Chansey at 357 was worth more than Alakazam at 12%, and the arithmetic proved it — Alakazam contributed nothing for the rest of the match while Chansey's absence lost it.

A distant second: I would spend Vaporeon's turn 12 on Protect rather than Scald. I reasoned that I was behind on bodies and needed damage, but the Scald was a coin-flip to even resolve and it lost the flip on a max roll. Protect was free, drained a Thunderbolt PP, and kept the 237-HP body alive one turn longer — and with everything else already one-shot, that body was worth more than 63 chip damage that never landed.

The structural lesson, which is not really a mistake so much as a matchup: this Bulwark build has no special-defensive answer to a high-power Electric attack once Chansey is gone. Four of six members are Water-typed or Water-adjacent. Against a Specs Jolteon that is not a wall, it is a queue.
