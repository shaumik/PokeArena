# sf1 — Solaris vs The Apothecary (Semifinal)

## Verdict

**Solaris wins, 4–0, on turn 19 of 60.** A wipe, by attrition rather than a
single sweep: Solaris lost Ninetales and Victreebel and finished with Charizard
untouched at 154/154, Exeggutor 94/171 (paralysed), Arcanine 67/166 and Venusaur
33/156. The Apothecary lost all six — Jynx (t3), Golbat (t8), Gengar (t10),
Zapdos (t15), Arbok (t18), Raticate (t19). Solaris goes to the final against
Hairtrigger.

## The story

The Apothecary opened by spending its Focus Sash lead exactly as the archetype
demands — Lovely Kiss into the Drought setter on turn 1 — and the engine handed
the turn straight back: Solaris pivoted Ninetales to the bench on turn 2, which
zeroes the sleep counter, and it woke on its first action back with the nap
having cost it nothing (see BUGS #1). From there the status team never got a
second grip. Golbat died on turn 8 to a +2 Fire Blast fired on the last turn of
the first sun — Solaris reading the eight-turn Heat Rock clock precisely and
cashing it on the final tick — and then came **the turning point, turn 10**:
Solaris let a 1-HP, Focus-Sash-spent Victreebel take a Sucker Punch into Gengar,
converting its most worthless body into the opponent's fastest attacker and going
6–3 up without having lost a single Pokémon. The Apothecary's remaining three
were all slower than a sunlit Solaris and none of them raced. Zapdos put up the
only real resistance, walling Exeggutor behind a 99-point Roost until a 10% Sp.
Def drop off Psychic on turn 14 broke the maths in one roll; it then wasted its
dying action on turn 15 Toxicking a target it had itself paralysed the turn
before, and Exeggutor finished it. Arbok traded Ninetales for itself, Raticate's
Flame Orb lit two turns before it had anything to hit, and Charizard walked in on
turn 17 at full HP and took the last two without being touched.

## Scorecard

- **Solaris** — **yes, and it played the archetype better than its predecessor
  did.** It set the sun three times, tracked the Heat Rock clock to the exact
  turn, and cashed the sky rather than admiring it: the turn-8 Golbat kill was
  fired on sun turn 8 of 8, and Chlorophyll won it the turn order on turns 5
  (Venusaur 264 over Gengar 178), 13 and 14 (Exeggutor 214). **Best decision:
  turn 9–10**, switching a full-HP Victreebel into a Sludge Wave it knew the
  Focus Sash would survive, then cashing the 1-HP body on a Sucker Punch read
  that was risk-free by construction — *"if Gengar instead clicks a status move,
  Sucker Punch whiffs but Victreebel takes zero damage. There is no line where
  this loses me anything."* That is the whole match in one turn. It then reused
  the same 1-HP body on turn 17 as a sacrifice to bring Charizard in clean.
  **Worst decision: turn 4**, switching Venusaur into Gengar on the claim that
  *"Venusaur is the one switch-in a status team cannot break."* Venusaur is
  Grass/**Poison** — Sludge Wave is 2 × 0.5 = neutral, not resisted — and it took
  123, i.e. 79% of the Pokémon that was supposed to be the wall. Venusaur landed
  one Sleep Powder in the entire match and then hid. Runner-up: the turn-5 pivot
  back to Ninetales *"to re-trigger Drought for 8 turns"* while the sun was still
  up, which extended nothing (BUGS #3).
  **Round 1 lessons: applied, explicitly.** Its predecessor lost the sun on turn
  8 of r1m2 and never got it back, and wrote *"a sun team that loses its Drought
  setter has to know immediately that it is no longer a sun team."* This pilot
  named that in its own reasoning on turn 5 — *"My predecessor lost this team's
  whole identity by never getting the sun back"* — protected Ninetales rather
  than trading it, got a second sun up on turn 11, and when it did lose Ninetales
  on turn 12 it pivoted instantly to type-advantage play (Exeggutor's Psychic
  into two Poison-types) exactly as the after-action report prescribed. It also
  learned the Drought no-refresh rule *inside the match*: turn 11's note reads
  *"Sun has actually lapsed now, so Drought will really fire this time."*

- **The Apothecary** — **yes, it played status, and status was the wrong weapon
  in this engine.** It opened with sleep, and inside twelve turns it had also
  landed Glare (paralysing Ninetales), Thunder Wave (paralysing Exeggutor), a
  Confuse Ray attempt, a Poison Fang and a self-inflicted Flame Orb burn. The
  prescriptions landed; the arithmetic never got to collect, because two of the
  three status axes the theme names are broken or blunted in this build (BUGS #1
  and #2) and the third — paralysis — is the one that helps least against a team
  that doubles its own Speed. **Best decision: turn 11's Glare into the
  switch-in.** It correctly predicted Solaris would bring Ninetales back for the
  sun, put paralysis on it, halved 167 to 83, and used the new turn order to
  Earthquake the Drought engine off the board on turn 12 — the cleanest two-turn
  sequence either side produced. **Worst decision: turn 15's Toxic.** Zapdos was
  on 21 HP and dead to the next Psychic; Roost was the only move that could
  survive the turn. It chose Toxic into an Exeggutor it had itself paralysed one
  turn earlier — a Pokémon already carrying a non-volatile status can never take
  a second one, so the move was *guaranteed* to fail before it was clicked. The
  log reads `Zapdos used Toxic! / But it failed!` and Zapdos died in the same
  turn having done nothing. Runner-up: turn 18's switch to Arbok to preserve
  Raticate's Facade, which fed Arbok to a Charizard that outsped it and killed it
  on entry.
  **Round 1 lessons: partially applied.** Its predecessor's report contains the
  sentence *"Facade does not double off my own burn in this engine… Raticate was
  roughly half as threatening as the archetype advertises,"* discovered on turn
  19 of r1m3 and written up in full. This pilot brought Raticate in on turn 15
  planning around *"Guts Facade rolls 151–190"* and was still quoting 162–190 on
  turn 19 — the exact figure its predecessor proved false. That is the one
  handover lesson it did not read. It did apply the other one: the predecessor
  warned that paralysis is situational, and this pilot correctly *did* reach for
  it here, where halving a Chlorophyll sweeper's Speed is the right call.

## MVP

**Exeggutor.** It came in on Ninetales' corpse on turn 12 into the Arbok that had
just killed it, and immediately made Arbok unplayable — a 2× Psychic from behind
214 Chlorophyll Speed, non-contact so Rocky Helmet never bit, and Grass resisting
the Earthquake that had done the killing. Arbok fled on turn 13 and The
Apothecary never willingly sent it back. It then took Zapdos — the designated
wall, the Pokémon Solaris itself wrote off as unbeatable (*"I cannot win a
93-per-turn grind against a 99-point Roost"*) — from 100% to dead across three
Psychics on turns 13, 14 and 15, breaking the Roost war on a 10% Sp. Def drop and
finishing it while paralysed and slower. Then it ate a Guts Crunch on turn 16 and
walked away on 55%. Two of The Apothecary's best four bodies, from a Pokémon that
spent most of its line at half speed. Honourable mention to **Charizard**, which
entered on turn 17 and took Arbok and Raticate without losing a single HP.

## Fix verification

**The faint-window patch (`9afa363`) held. Nothing downstream of it regressed.**

I probed every branch the patch touches and every neighbour it could plausibly
have broken, 120–400 seeds apiece, alongside watching live play:

| Path | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Foe secondary on a target the same hit killed | `TestSecondaryDoesNotLandOnDyingTarget` passes; 0/400 | fixed |
| Poison Touch on a lethal contact hit | corpse poisoned **0/200** | fixed — the ability-side twin of the same bug is clean |
| Drain move (Giga Drain) landing the KO | attacker healed **120/120**, victim left alive **0/120** | correct — canon drains off damage dealt even on a KO; the guard did not suppress it |
| Recoil (Flare Blitz) on a lethal hit | recoil applied **120/120** | unaffected |
| Rocky Helmet vs a lethal contact hit | attacker chipped **120/120** | unaffected |
| Self-targeted secondary (Close Combat's −1/−1) on a lethal hit | applied **120/120** | unaffected — deleting the `sec.Self && atk.Fainted` guard did not over-fire |
| Contact rider on a holder the hit killed (Static) | fired **93/300 ≈ 31%** | correct — canon runs `DamagingHit` before `faintMessages`; the patch did not over-suppress |

Live corroboration, **turn 8**: Ninetales' +2 Fire Blast took Golbat from 182/182
to 0 and Golbat's **Sitrus Berry did not fire**. `applyItemHPTrigger`
(`internal/engine/items.go:626`) guards on `p.Fainted || p.HP <= 0`, so the
"heal berry resurrects a corpse" member of the same bug family is closed at that
site too. No secondary, drain, recoil or reactive item behaved anomalously in any
of the 19 turns.

**One hardening note, not a live defect.** The patch guarded the *callers* in
`applyDamageEffects`, not the sink. `inflictStatus` (`effects.go:511`) still
opens `if p.Status != StatusNone || p.Fainted`, and `applyItemStatusCure`
(`items.go:677`) still opens `if p == nil || p.Fainted` — both read the flag, not
the HP, and both sit inside the faint window whenever a contact rider or a
Synchronize bounce reaches them. Nothing reached them this match, because the new
upstream guards stop it. But the next effect wired into that window
reintroduces the bug for free. Routing those two through `isDown()` as well would
make the invariant hold at the sink instead of at each caller — which is what the
`turn.go:869` comment actually asks for.

## BUGS

### 1. Switching a sleeping Pokémon out cures its sleep — CONFIRMED (high severity, shaped this match)

**What I saw.** Turn 1: `Jynx used Lovely Kiss! / Ninetales was put to sleep!`
Turn 2, Solaris switches Ninetales out for Arcanine. Ninetales sits on the bench
through turns 2–5, returns turn 6, and turn 7 opens `Ninetales woke up!` — then
uses Nasty Plot the same turn. Sleep cost it zero actions. The Apothecary did the
same on turn 8 with a Gengar slept on turn 5 (`Gengar woke up!`, turn 9). Both
pilots named the mechanic outright: Solaris — *"wakes on entry because the
earlier pivot zeroed its sleep counter"*; The Apothecary — *"Gengar switched out
asleep, which zeroes its sleep counter — it is guaranteed to wake on its first
move attempt and still act, exactly as their Ninetales did on turn 7."*

**What I expected.** Sleep is inflicted for `rng.Range(2, 4)` turns, so Ninetales'
counter was 2 at minimum. With the counter preserved across the bench, its first
action back decrements 2 → 1 and it stays asleep; it is arithmetically impossible
for it to wake on turn 7. In canon, switching out never cures sleep in any
generation: Gen 5+ preserves the counter, and the older Gen 1–4 "counter reset"
meant a *fresh full duration* on return — strictly worse for the sleeper, never
better.

**What the source says.** `doSwitchWithCarry` (`internal/engine/switching.go:47`):

```go
if out.Status == StatusSleep {
    out.SleepTurns = 0
}
```

bare, with no explanatory comment — unusual in a file where every deliberate
decision carries a paragraph. `canAct` (`internal/engine/turn.go:1441`) then
treats zero as the wake sentinel:

```go
case StatusSleep:
    if p.SleepTurns > 0 { p.SleepTurns--; ... }
    if p.SleepTurns <= 0 {
        p.Status = StatusNone
        *log = append(*log, LogLine{... p.Name + " woke up!"})
        return true
    }
```

Zeroing the counter *is* clearing the status, one action later. Reproduced in
isolation (`TestJudgeProbeSleepPivot`): status `sleep` / turns `3` → switch out →
turns `0`, status still `sleep` → switch back in → first action logs
`Ninetales woke up!` and the move resolves normally.

**On the "it's documented" defence.** `docs/battle-state.md:163` says "Counter
resets on switch-out" and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:294` calls it "sleep with
**Gen-5+ switch reset**". That label is backwards — Gen 5 is precisely the
generation that *removed* the switch reset. And read as an approximation of the
Gen 1–4 rule it is implemented inside-out: "reset" there means re-roll a duration,
not set the counter to the value that means *awake*. The docs record an intent;
the code implements the opposite of that intent's canonical effect. Note also the
asymmetry with the sibling field, which the r1m3 referee raised: the same function
does **not** reset `ToxicCounter`, where canon says it should. The engine resets
exactly the counter canon preserves and preserves exactly the counter canon
resets.

**Why it matters here.** This is a knockout match against a team whose declared
archetype opens with the word "Sleep". The Apothecary led Jynx and spent the one
action its Focus Sash guaranteed on Lovely Kiss into the opposing weather setter.
Under canon that is 1–3 turns of a free-swinging Ninetales; here it bought a
single switch. Solaris then reused the trick to shed its own liability, and on
turn 8 collected a second dividend: a benched Pokémon that still *reads* `sleep`
keeps Sleep Clause locked against the foe while paying nothing for it. Both sides
used it, so the match was not unfair — but an entire status axis is effectively
deleted, and it is deleted asymmetrically against the archetype built on it.
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

### 2. Facade does not double off the user's own status — CONFIRMED (reported in r1m3, still unpatched)

**What I saw.** Raticate lit its Flame Orb on turn 16 and clicked Facade twice,
turns 17 and 19, with The Apothecary asserting *"Flame Orb lit, so Guts is live
and Facade doubles to 140 off my own burn… roughly 267"* (t17) and *"Guts Facade
rolls 162–190 into Charizard's 154 and OHKOs it"* (t19). Neither hit gave a clean
magnitude reading — turn 17's Facade landed on a 1-HP Victreebel and reported
`Victreebel took 1 damage`, and turn 19 never resolved because Charizard outsped
163 with 167 and killed Raticate first. So this match produced no *live*
measurement, only the plans built on the false premise.

**What I expected.** Facade's defining property is base power 70 → 140 when the
user is burned, poisoned or paralysed (and, from Gen 6, it also ignores burn's
Attack cut). The Apothecary's team file pairs Facade with a Flame Orb and Guts
specifically to cash this, and the trainer theme says so: *"Facade doubles off the
team's own burn."*

**What the source says.** `grep -rn "facade" --include=*.go internal/` returns
nothing — Facade is not referenced anywhere in the engine. The only dynamic
base-power rewriter is `applyCallbackPower`
(`internal/engine/callbackmoves.go:282`), and its `statusDoublingMoves` map
(line 257) has exactly two entries, `hex` and `venoshock`, both keyed on the
**defender**. `data/moves.json` carries Facade as a flat 70-BP Normal physical
move with no marker. Nothing anywhere reads the attacker's status for base power.

**Measured** (`TestJudgeProbeFacade`, Raticate → Snorlax, ability and item
stripped so only base power varies): clean **51**, poisoned **51**, burned **27**.
Poison should roughly double the clean figure and returns it unchanged; burn
returns roughly *half*, because the engine applies the burn Attack halve
(`damage.go:359`) to a move that in canon both doubles and ignores it. Guts itself
is fine — verified at ×3.0 (`abilities.go:1226`), and confirmed independently by
the r1m3 damage numbers — so with Raticate's real kit the effect is a Facade
firing at 70 BP where the pilot plans around 140. Against Charizard's 154 HP that
is roughly 87–103 instead of 174–206: the difference between a chip and the OHKO
The Apothecary spent its last two turns setting up.

**Status.** Raised as CONFIRMED by the r1m3 referee with the same arithmetic and
the same one-line fix sketch (add `facade` to `statusDoublingMoves`, widening the
map's `func(def *Pokemon) bool` signature to also take the attacker). The
Round-1-to-semifinal patch shipped the faint-window fix but not this one, so it
has now been live for two tournament matches in a row — this time in a knockout,
on the team that reported it, and the new pilot planned around the canon numbers
because the handover note never reached it. **Verdict: CONFIRMED (known,
accepted, unpatched).**

### 3. A weather-keyed end-of-turn ability skips its tick on the weather's final turn — CONFIRMED (low severity)

**What I saw.** Turn 18. Charizard (Solar Power) was active for the whole turn,
the sun was up — `The sunlight faded.` prints at the *end* of turn 18 — and there
is no `Charizard was hurt by Solar Power!` line anywhere in the log. `state.json`
confirms Charizard finished at 154/154, untouched. Turn 18 was the only sunlit
end-of-turn Charizard ever saw (it entered on turn 17's replace phase and the sun
died on 18), so the 1/8 chip never landed once all match.

**What I expected.** Solar Power chips 1/8 max HP at end of turn while the sun is
up. Sun was up for the whole of turn 18, and the engine's own weather-chip
residual treats it as up on its final turn.

**What the source says.** The residual block in `ResolveTurn` runs
`applyWeatherResidual` (turn.go:164) → `applyItemHPTriggers` → **`tickWeather`
(turn.go:165)**, and only much later `applyAbilityEndOfTurn` (turn.go:229–230).
`tickWeather` (`residuals.go:201`) decrements and, at zero, sets `s.Weather = nil`.
Solar Power's `EndOfTurn` (`abilities.go:606`) then asks
`weatherFor(..., effectiveWeather(s))` and sees nothing.

**The part that makes it a defect rather than an off-by-one preference.** The
same residual phase answers "is the weather still up?" two different ways:

```
sandstorm, LAST turn:  weather chip=9  | ... | Charizard is buffeted by the sandstorm! (-9) | The sandstorm subsided.
sun,       LAST turn:  ability chip=0  | ... | The sunlight faded.
```

(`TestJudgeProbeFinalWeatherTurnConsistency`.) The weather's own chip lands on the
final turn because it runs *before* `tickWeather`; every weather-keyed ability
tick is skipped because it runs *after*. In canon these cannot disagree — Showdown
drives both from the same weather condition's `onFieldResidual` →
`eachEvent('Weather')`, so whatever the off-by-one, the chip and the ability fire
or skip together. Here they are on opposite sides of the countdown. The same
skip applies to Dry Skin, Rain Dish, Ice Body and Hydration.

**Severity.** Charizard avoided one 19-HP chip in a match it won 4–0 with that
Charizard at full HP; the result is unaffected. But it is a systematic one-tick
underpayment for every weather-ability holder, it always favours the weather
team, and the fix is a move rather than a rewrite: run `applyAbilityEndOfTurn`
before `tickWeather`, or snapshot the weather for the residual phase.
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**, low severity.

### 4. Stale comment on `setWeatherFromAbility` — NOT-A-BUG (code correct, comment wrong)

**What I saw.** Turn 5, Solaris switched Ninetales back in explicitly to reset the
weather clock — *"Ninetales re-triggers Drought for 8 turns off Heat Rock"* — and
the turn-6 log carries no `Ninetales's ability set the weather!` line at all. The
sun kept its turn-1 clock and expired on schedule at the end of turn 8. Solaris
spent a turn on nothing, then worked the rule out for itself and got it right on
turn 11 (*"Sun has actually lapsed now, so Drought will really fire this time"* —
and it did).

**What the source says.** `setWeatherFromAbility` (`abilities.go:1303`) opens with
`if s.Weather != nil && s.Weather.Kind == kind { return }` — a hard no-op when the
same weather is already up. That is canon: Showdown's `setWeather` returns false
for a same-weather set, so a Drought holder re-entering its own sun extends
nothing. Confirmed by `TestJudgeProbeDroughtReentry`: sun at `TurnsLeft: 2`,
Drought holder re-enters, still `TurnsLeft: 2`.

**But** the doc comment three lines above says the opposite: *"an ability
auto-setter never 'fails' when the same weather is already up; it just refreshes
to the default duration silently when it would be a no-op."* It does not refresh;
it returns. **Verdict: NOT-A-BUG**, but worth a one-line correction — a pilot
reading this engine could reasonably build a line on that sentence, and one
effectively did.

### Everything else checked clean

Audited against `internal/engine/*.go`, `data/moves.json`, `data/pokedex.json`,
`data/typechart.json` and `data/items.json` across all 19 turns:

- **Drought / Heat Rock.** Fired on Ninetales' turn-0 entry and again on its
  turn-11 re-entry (once the sun had genuinely lapsed). `weatherTurnsFor` →
  `extendedFieldTurns = 8` (`items_field.go:70`). Sun 1 ran turns 1–8, sun 2 ran
  turns 11–18, `The sunlight faded.` at the end of each. Exactly 8 both times.
- **Sun damage multipliers.** `damageMultByType`: Fire ×1.5, Water ×0.5. Turn 8's
  +2 Fire Blast into Golbat computes to a 263–309 raw band against a 182-HP
  target — consistent only with the ×1.5 applied.
- **Chlorophyll.** `abilities.go:1194`, ×2 in sun, ×1 otherwise, read through
  `weatherFor`. Measured Venusaur 132 → 264 and Exeggutor 107 → 214, both
  matching the pilots' own figures, and confirmed live by turn 5 (Venusaur before
  a 178-Speed Gengar) and turn 13. Correctly *removed* off the sun: Victreebel
  needed Sucker Punch's +1 priority on turn 10 rather than raw Speed.
- **Chlorophyll + paralysis stacking.** Turn 14 onward, Exeggutor at 107 —
  `effectiveSpeed` (`damage.go:85`) applies stages, then the paralysis ×0.5, then
  the ability ×2 (107 → 53.5 → 107), which is why Zapdos at 120 took the turn
  order for the rest of its life. Correct.
- **Solar Beam charge skip.** `skipChargeTurn` (`turn.go:1096`) short-circuits for
  `solar-beam`/`solar-blade` in sun only, ahead of the Power Herb branch so a herb
  is never spent for free. Never exercised live — but note Solaris' turn-19
  reasoning, *"Solar Beam would need a charge turn now that the sun is down,"*
  which is the rule stated correctly by a player who had it available.
- **Solar Power.** ×1.5 on special moves in sun (measured 51 → 76) and 1/8 chip
  (measured 19 off 153). The boost could not be isolated live — turn 18's crit Air
  Slash was clamped to Arbok's exact 167 HP with or without it. The chip is BUGS
  #3.
- **Status residual arithmetic.** `residuals.go:26–33`. Live: Raticate's burn
  ticked **8** off 131 max HP (131/16 = 8.19) on turns 17 and 18. Poison `MaxHP/8`
  and toxic `MaxHP × counter / 16` verified by inspection; no poison or toxic ever
  landed in this match (Poison Fang connected on turn 7 but its 50% bad-poison
  secondary did not roll; Toxic failed on turn 15 for the reason below).
- **Burn's Attack cut and Guts.** `damage.go:359` halves the physical side of the
  ratio, keyed on category not stat; `guts` (`abilities.go:1226`) returns ×3.0
  when burned (×1.5 boost × ×2.0 cancelling the halve) and ×1.5 for any other
  status. Both correct — the defect is only in Facade's base power.
- **Hex / Venoshock.** `statusDoublingMoves` — Hex doubles against any
  non-volatile status, Venoshock only against `StatusPoison`/`StatusToxic`.
  Measured Hex 57 → 111 against a paralysed target; Venoshock doubles for poison
  and toxic but correctly not for burn. Neither refuses-to-double case regressed.
  Neither move was clicked in this match (Gengar died with Hex unused, Arbok with
  Venoshock unused).
- **Sleep Clause.** `sleepClauseBlocks` (`clauses.go:142`) walks the whole team
  including the bench, and is reachable only from `inflictStatusFrom`, so Rest is
  exempt by call graph rather than by a flag. Probed:
  `Gengar stayed awake! (Sleep Clause) / But it failed!`. Never triggered live —
  each side only ever had one sleeper at a time.
- **One-status-at-a-time.** Turn 15's `Zapdos used Toxic! / But it failed!` is
  correct, not a missing Poison immunity: Exeggutor is Grass/Psychic and perfectly
  poisonable, but it was already paralysed, and `inflictStatus` (`effects.go:511`)
  refuses when `p.Status != StatusNone`. The Apothecary's own Thunder Wave one turn
  earlier is what blocked its own Toxic.
- **Status immunities.** `inflictStatus` (`effects.go:520–535`) refuses burn on
  Fire, freeze on Ice, paralysis on Electric, poison/toxic on Poison **and**
  Steel, after the ability, ability-state and terrain guards. Relevant and
  correct here: Ninetales is Fire and took paralysis from Glare (legal) but could
  never have been burned; Venusaur and Victreebel are Poison-types and were never
  poisonable, which is why Zapdos' Toxic was pointed at Exeggutor in the first
  place.
- **Confusion.** 33% self-hit (`turn.go:1426`), 40-BP typeless physical, no
  STAB/crit/type, burn does not halve it — the modern Gen-7+ rate. Golbat's
  Confuse Ray on turn 8 never resolved (KO'd first).
- **Speed order.** Level 50 throughout, and every ordering decision checked out:
  turn 1 Ninetales 167 over Jynx 161 (The Apothecary's opening note *"Jynx
  outspeeds Ninetales at 161"* was its own miscalculation, not an engine fault);
  turn 3 Extreme Speed at priority +2 over a faster Jynx; turn 10 Sucker Punch at
  +1 over a 178-Speed Gengar; turn 12 a paralysed Ninetales at 83 behind Arbok's
  100; turn 19 Charizard 167 over Raticate 163.
- **Sucker Punch's condition.** `turn.go:578` fails the move against a target that
  already moved, is switching, is using a status move, or is recharging. It
  succeeded on turn 10 (Gengar had Sludge Wave queued) and would have failed on
  turn 11 against Glare — which is exactly what The Apothecary was playing for
  when it clicked Glare. Correct on both sides of the read.
- **Type effectiveness.** Turn 1 Fire→Ice/Psychic 2×; turn 3 Normal→Ghost 0
  (which is why Gengar entered free against a Choice-locked Extreme Speed); turn 4
  Poison→Grass/Poison **1×** (2 × 0.5 — no effectiveness line printed, and 123
  damage matches the neutral band; a 2× read would have been 216–255, and *both*
  pilots wrote "2× on Venusaur"); turn 10 Dark→Ghost/Poison 2×; turn 12
  Ground→Fire 2×; turn 16 Dark→Grass/Psychic 2×; turn 18 Flying→Poison 2×. All
  agree with `data/typechart.json`.
