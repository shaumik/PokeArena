# r1m2 — Solaris (p2) vs Spike Cartel (p1)

**Result: WIN. 3–0.** Battle ended turn 18 (turn cap 60).

Solaris survivors: Exeggutor 104/171, Venusaur 121/156, Charizard 53/154.
Losses: Ninetales, Victreebel, Arcanine.
Spike Cartel: wiped — Omastar, Muk, Gengar, Rhydon, Cloyster, Golbat all fainted.

## Game plan

Turn on the sky, then cash it before it runs out. Concretely:

1. **Lead Ninetales** so Drought is up on turn 0 and Solar Beam fires without a charge turn.
2. **Absorb their gimmick.** Spike Cartel wanted hazards; I had three Grass/Poison bodies that laugh at Toxic Spikes and a Heavy-Duty-Boots Charizard. Hazard chip was never going to be my losing condition.
3. **Win the type war.** A rocks-and-spikes roster is structurally Rock/Ground/Water/Steel-heavy — which is to say it is a buffet for a team of Fire and 4x-effective Grass moves.

It survived contact, but not intact. Two things went wrong early:

- **Turn 1: Golbat's Toxic landed on Ninetales.** That put a hard clock on my weather setter before it had done more than one turn of work.
- **Turn 4: Muk's Knock Off removed the Heat Rock.** With Ninetales dying to Toxic on the same turn, that was the moment the sun became a one-time resource instead of a renewable one. Sun ran out on turn 8 and never came back — half my creed (doubled Chlorophyll speed, one-turn Solar Beam, Solar Power) was offline for the last ten turns.

So I pivoted: stop playing a weather team, start playing a **type-advantage** team. Grass STAB alone was 4x on Omastar, 4x on Rhydon, and 4x on Cloyster. I won on the typechart, not on the sky.

## The turn that decided the match

**Turn 7 — Growth into the last ray of sunlight.**

Sun was at 1 turn, Ninetales was dead, and I knew Drought could never come back. Golbat was in front of me with Toxic already revealed — and Venusaur is a Poison-type, so Toxic could not touch it. I spent the final sunlit turn on Growth, which in sun gives **+2** instead of +1.

That single boost turned a resisted Sludge Bomb into a 119-damage hit on a Poison/Flying Golbat, which outpaced its own 91-HP Roost. Golbat panicked and Whirlwinded — and the drag-out handed me Charizard, the exact Pokémon I would have chosen, who then took 86 off Rhydon and killed Gengar on consecutive turns. The whole middle of the game unspooled from converting one expiring turn of weather into a permanent stat.

Runner-up: **turn 14**, Victreebel's Power Whip into Cloyster. It traded down (Skill Link Icicle Spear is 2x on Grass and killed through my Focus Sash), but it left Cloyster on 50 HP and — crucially — Victreebel moved *first*, which told me Cloyster was under 122 speed. That read let me send Venusaur in confident it would out-speed and finish the job with a 100%-accurate Giga Drain.

## One thing I would do differently

**Turn 12: I switched Arcanine into a live Rhydon and it was OHKO'd by Stone Edge on the switch-in.**

I pivoted off a 34% Charizard specifically to dodge a 4x Rock move — and then brought in a *Fire*-type, which is 2x weak to Rock. I let "Intimidate plus 166 HP feels bulky" override the typechart, and Intimidate does nothing about a super-effective crit. Rhydon was on 9% HP; **Victreebel was the correct switch** — faster than Rhydon, Focus Sash as insurance, and a 4x Power Whip that (as it did one turn later) kills for 22 damage. I threw away a healthy Pokémon for zero reason and it very nearly cost me the Cloyster endgame, since Arcanine's banded Close Combat was my cleanest answer to a Water/Ice wall.

Broader lesson for the archetype: a sun team that loses its Heat Rock and its Drought setter in the same turn has to *know immediately* that it is no longer a sun team. I made that adjustment with Venusaur's Growth, but I forgot it again four turns later with Arcanine.
