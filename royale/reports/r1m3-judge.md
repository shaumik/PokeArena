# r1m3 — Meridian (SUN) vs Miasma (STATUS) · seed 8093

## Verdict

**Meridian wins, 3–0** (Vileplume, Exeggutor and Flareon never had to leave the bench), in 15 turns of a 60-turn cap.

Miasma spent its whole game plan on paralysis and never got a second status to stick — its own Thunder Wave locked Butterfree's Sleep Powder out on the one-status rule. Rapidash then walked through four Pokémon in a row and died to its own Flare Blitz recoil on the same turn it killed the last one.

## The story

Meridian's opening was the entire thesis of the archetype: Ninetales in, Drought up, eight turns on the clock courtesy of Heat Rock, and a Fire Blast already in the air before Miasma had finished switching Parasect out of the way. Hypno ate 79 of it and went to work on the only thing that actually threatened the sun team — the sun team's speed. Thunder Wave landed on turn 2, right after Ninetales banked a Nasty Plot, and for a moment the match looked like the status player's script: cripple the sweeper, outlive the weather, win the long game.

It never got to the long game. A +2 Fire Blast at noon is not a move you wall with a body, and Muk — brought in specifically to be that body, Assault Vest and all — took 157 and then died to Psyshock without ever throwing the Gunk Shot it was sent in for. Butterfree came in behind it and ran straight into the ugliest interaction of the match: Sleep Powder, 97% accurate through Compound Eyes, *failed*, because Ninetales was already paralyzed. Miasma had spent its own clock on Thunder Wave and paid for it by locking its best sleep move out of the game. Butterfree's Focus Sash bought exactly one turn, which it spent on a 30-damage Bug Buzz, and then it was gone too.

Arbok was the one thing Miasma got right. It walked in, Intimidated, ate a Psyshock that never came, and Gunk Shot took Ninetales out at full-ish health. Then it Coiled through a paralyzed Moltres's turn, ate a 114 Fire Blast down to 16%, and one-shot the bird with the boosted barrel. Two kills from 136 HP. But the sun had already gone out on turn 8 and Meridian didn't need it any more — Rapidash, jolly, 172 Speed, Charcoal, came in faster than everything Miasma had left.

What followed was demolition. High Horsepower into a Hypno switch, Flare Blitz to finish Hypno, Flare Blitz through Parasect's 4× Fire weakness for a clean OHKO (paying 78 HP to Rocky Helmet and recoil for the privilege — Parasect never got Spore off, either), High Horsepower to mop up the 22 HP Arbok had left, and finally Flare Blitz into Venomoth, which had just spent its only turn on a Quiver Dance it would never use. Venomoth died; Rapidash's recoil killed Rapidash. Both bodies hit the floor on turn 15, and Meridian had three Pokémon still standing.

## Scorecard

**Meridian (SUN) — A–.** Executed the archetype's fast half perfectly and never needed the slow half. Drought went up turn 0 and the Heat Rock gave a clean eight turns; the sun-boosted Fire Blast is directly visible in the numbers (Muk's 157 sits in the sun range [138,163] and nowhere near the no-sun range [92,109]). Nasty Plot on turn 1 into a Thunder Wave was the one loose read — it cost tempo against a Pokémon that was never going to threaten Ninetales — but it also bought the +2 that vaporised Muk and Butterfree, so it paid. Marked down only because half the theme never fired: Chlorophyll, Harvest, Solar Beam and the whole Vileplume/Exeggutor engine sat on the bench, and the win came from raw Fire STAB plus a Charcoal horse, not from the sun package. Rapidash's 4-KO run was clean sequencing — recoil moves only when the maths said the kill was there, High Horsepower when it didn't.

**Miasma (STATUS) — C+.** The plan was sound and the execution self-sabotaged. Two paralyses landed (Ninetales turn 2, Moltres turn 8) and both mattered — Ninetales at half speed lost the initiative to Butterfree, and Moltres at 78 Speed lost it to Arbok, which is the whole reason Arbok got two kills. But the one-status rule is the load-bearing constraint on a roster carrying Spore, Hypnosis, Sleep Powder, Stun Spore, Glare, Thunder Wave and Toxic, and Miasma kept aiming a second status at bodies it had already statused. Spore was never cast (Parasect died before it moved, twice over). Toxic was never cast. Poison Touch never fired because Muk never moved. Rocky Helmet and Effect Spore got exactly one contact hit to work with. Arbok was the only Pokémon on the side that converted, and it converted brilliantly: Intimidate twice, Coil once, two kills, and a Glare that stuck.

## MVP

**Rapidash (Meridian).** Four KOs in five turns of field time — Hypno, Parasect, Arbok, Venomoth — starting from a switch-in at 100% and ending at 0 from its own recoil. It killed Parasect (4× weak, but holding Rocky Helmet and Effect Spore) at a cost of 78 HP in one turn and kept swinging at 24%. It also took Intimidate on the chin at turn 13, dropping to 101 effective Attack, and still cleared Arbok and Venomoth with the reduced number. Runner-up is Arbok, which got two kills out of 136 HP, but Rapidash closed the match.

## BUGS

**CONFIRMED (latent — did not affect this match).** Effect Spore does not respect powder immunity. Since Gen VI, Effect Spore cannot affect Grass-types, Overcoat holders, or Safety Goggles holders — it is treated as a powder effect. The engine's implementation at `internal/engine/abilities.go:837-855` checks only `moveMakesContact` and the 30% roll, then routes straight into `inflictStatusFrom`; its caller `applyOnHit` (`internal/engine/abilities.go:1806-1814`) applies no powder filter either. The powder-immunity helper `powderRefusedBy` (`internal/engine/items.go:1029-1043`) is gated on `m.HasFlag("powder")`, so it is unreachable from an ability rider and is never consulted here. No test in `internal/engine/*_test.go` covers Effect Spore against a Grass-type. This never triggered live — the only contact move Parasect absorbed was Rapidash's Flare Blitz (turn 13), and Rapidash is Fire — but Meridian's bench held Vileplume (Grass/Poison) and Exeggutor (Grass/Psychic), either of which would have exposed it. Reporting it because the brief specifically asked whether Effect Spore respects status immunities; it respects the type/ability/one-status guards inside `inflictStatus`, but not the powder clause.

**NOT-A-BUG — Sleep Powder "But it failed!" on turn 5.** Correct. Ninetales was already paralyzed and `inflictStatus` refuses any second major status at `internal/engine/effects.go:518` (`if p.Status != StatusNone || isDown(p) { return false }`). The message is the right one — Showdown emits `-fail` for a status move whose status cannot apply, not a miss. Accuracy was never rolled, so Compound Eyes was not implicated.

**NOT-A-BUG — the weather chain, end to end.** Heat Rock gave exactly 8 turns: the report snapshots read `sun(7)` after turn 1 down to `sun(1)` after turn 7, cleared at end of turn 8 (`internal/engine/weather.go:40` `defaultWeatherTurns = 5`, extended via `weatherTurnsFor` at `internal/engine/items.go:988`, item registered at `internal/engine/items_field.go:204`). Moltres's post-sun Fire Blast on turn 9 landed at 114, inside the unboosted band [112,132]. Drought fires on switch-in via `setWeatherFromAbility` (`internal/engine/abilities.go:1331-1347`) and refreshes rather than failing when the same sky is already up.

**NOT-A-BUG — weather-keyed residual abilities on the weather's final turn.** The previously-fixed defect still holds. `tickWeather` is invoked at `internal/engine/turn.go:238`, *after* both `applyAbilityEndOfTurn` calls at `turn.go:230-231`, with a comment at `turn.go:233-237` naming exactly the regression ("the sky went out from under Solar Power, Dry Skin, Rain Dish, Ice Body and Hydration on the weather's final turn"). **NOT-A-BUG — damage rounding at every modifier boundary.** `computeDamage` assembles in Showdown's exact order — `base+2` → weather group → crit (bare truncated multiply) → randomizer `tr(dmg*r/100)` → STAB → type chart → single chained final-modifier group (`internal/engine/damage.go:307-345`), with `chainMod`/`applyMod`/`applyTypeEffectiveness` at `damage.go:618-660`. I re-derived every damage figure in the match under that order and all twelve landed inside the 85–100 band: 79 ∈ [69,82], 157 ∈ [138,163], 30 ∈ [30,36] (minimum roll), 114 ∈ [112,132], 61 ∈ [60,71], plus five values capped at the target's remaining HP. **NOT-A-BUG — Compound Eyes and the accuracy cap.** `AccuracyMult: 1.3` at `internal/engine/abilities.go:579-582`, applied at `internal/engine/turn.go:1195` and clamped at `turn.go:1220-1222` (`if chance > 100 { chance = 100 }`). **NOT-A-BUG — Rocky Helmet before recoil (turn 13).** Log order was `Flare Blitz` → damage → `It's super effective!` → `Rocky Helmet (-23)` → `recoil (-55)` → `Parasect fainted!`. That is Showdown's order: the defender's damaging-hit item/ability riders resolve before the attacker's recoil, and the faint message resolves after both. Recoil arithmetic is also right — Flare Blitz recoil is 33% of *damage actually dealt*, so turn 12's capped 88 gave −29 and turn 15's capped kill gave −34 (Rapidash's remaining HP), not 33% of the uncapped roll.

**NOT-A-BUG — secondaries inside the faint window.** `applyOnHit` gates on `def.Fainted` (`abilities.go:1808`), and `inflictStatus` gates on `isDown` (`effects.go:513-519`) with a comment naming that distinction. So Parasect's Effect Spore did get a genuine 30% roll on the fatal turn-13 Flare Blitz and simply lost it — consistent with Rocky Helmet firing on the same hit. Not a suppressed proc.

**NOT-A-BUG — Sleep Clause implementation.** Never exercised live (Spore was never cast; Hypnosis never cast; Sleep Powder failed on the status rule). Source-checked anyway per the brief: `sleepClauseBlocks` (`internal/engine/clauses.go:142-155`) scans the whole team including the bench, skips fainted Pokémon and the target itself, and is consulted only from the foe-induced path `inflictStatusFrom` (`internal/engine/effects.go:468-474`) so self-sleep from Rest is exempt by call-graph construction.
